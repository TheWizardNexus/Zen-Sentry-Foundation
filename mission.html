The mission

Awareness. Into protection.

Zen Sentry Foundation supports Veterans, first responders, high-trust professionals, and their families through early support, peer connection, resilience education, practical navigation, and ethical technology used under human oversight.

Veteran-owned & operatedHuman-centeredPrevention-oriented

The name is the method

Three commitments. One discipline.

The foundation’s name is not decorative. Each word defines how the work should feel and where its limits belong.

ZenComposure

Clarity under pressure, disciplined attention, and enough stillness to see what matters before acting.

SentryEarly awareness

Ethical vigilance and protection without force: notice change, preserve dignity, and bring support closer.

FoundationDurable support

Structures that serve people, families, and trusted institutions beyond a single conversation or crisis moment.

Operating principle

Technology can assist.
People remain responsible.

Systems may help organize reflection, identify a useful resource, document a handoff, or make program learning visible. They do not own a person’s story or decide what care means.

What the work strengthens

Earlier recognition, trusted conversations, reviewed resources, warm handoffs, practical next steps, accountable evaluation, and sustained human follow-through.

What the work does not replace

Licensed care, peer relationships, emergency response, informed consent, contextual judgment, or the authority of people affected by a consequential decision.

Protection is shared

Built with communities.
Not around them.

Responsible prevention work starts with the people who understand the culture, the institution, the lived experience, and the stakes.

  • Veterans and familiesPartners, leaders, evaluators, and community builders—not problems to solve.
  • Peer leadersTrusted connection grounded in shared experience and responsible boundaries.
  • Behavioral-health professionalsQualified care, clinical context, referral pathways, and professional judgment.
  • First respondersHigh-trust experience, operational reality, and support before strain becomes crisis.
  • Researchers and educatorsEvidence, cultural humility, program learning, and honest limits.
  • Responsible technologistsPrivacy, human authority, reviewability, and tools that strengthen connection.
