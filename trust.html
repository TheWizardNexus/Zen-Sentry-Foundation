Trust principles

Boundaries that
shape the build.

These are operating requirements, not decorative values. A program, research release, or technology claim should be able to show how each one is applied.

Human authority

Consequential choices remain contestable.

People affected by a decision, qualified professionals, peers, and accountable program leaders retain interpretation and decision authority.

Privacy

Public does not mean personal.

Private prompts, model responses, evaluator identities, Veteran records, health information, addresses, EIN material, and controlled program artifacts stay out of this public site.

Maturity

Building is not delivered.

Awarded is not completed. Answer-complete is not scored. A plan is not an outcome. Each stage should retain its own language.

Clinical boundary

Tools are not care.

Nothing here is therapy, diagnosis, emergency response, clinical validation, or a replacement for licensed professionals and qualified care.

Publication boundary

Useful transparency.
Responsible restraint.

Public information should make mission, method, funding, stage, aggregate findings, and practical pathways understandable without exposing private records or flattening human context.

Appropriate to publish

Mission, current program model, verified funding, maturity, methods, aggregate findings, source boundaries, public links, limitations, and future learning goals.

Controlled or excluded

Personal records, health data, private prompts and responses, evaluator identities, secure legal and tax records, residential addresses, credentials, and unreviewed detailed evidence.
