Grant-supported program

Built around the Veteran.

The Zen Sentry Veteran Resiliency & Early Support Program is building a human-led pathway designed to help Veterans reflect earlier, connect with trusted people, find reviewed resources, and complete meaningful next steps.

Confirmed milestone

Grant support.
Accountable build.

CenterPoint Energy Foundation selected Zen Sentry for a 2026 $6,000 Energizing Veteran Resilience Grant supporting the formally named program. Selection is a starting point—not proof that services or outcomes are complete.

What is supported today

A defined Veteran-centered pathway, a confirmed award, current program development, and a forward-looking set of learning goals.

What is not represented today

Completed delivery, participant outcomes, clinical effectiveness, autonomous care, or impact numbers not yet backed by service records.

One connected pathway

From reflection.
To a useful next step.

Four complementary capabilities are being shaped into one experience. Each supports connection; none is meant to stand alone as care.

01

PreCrisis

Selected structured-reflection capabilities are intended to help a Veteran notice change, organize what they are experiencing, and prepare for a meaningful conversation.

Earlier awareness creates more opportunity to choose support and plan the next step.

02

ZSF Veteran Resource Library

Reviewed information across mental health, benefits, housing, transportation, and basic needs is being organized on BOSS Library Core.

Clear information can support confident choices, warm handoffs, and stronger follow-through.

03

Warrior Spirit

A Veteran-centered experience for reflection, resilience planning, and connection with a peer, navigator, or trusted resource.

Shared experience can help restore perspective, purpose, and momentum.

04

Trusted human support

Peers, navigators, professionals, families, and community partners remain the people who interpret context, make decisions, and stay with the next step.

Technology strengthens the connection; it does not become the relationship.

Program learning

What success will look like.

These are intended learning targets, written in future tense until delivery evidence exists. Program reporting should make progress and gaps visible without overstating causation.

Clear boundary

Support before crisis.
Not crisis response.

The program is not therapy, diagnosis, emergency response, or a replacement for licensed professionals, qualified care, or human judgment.
